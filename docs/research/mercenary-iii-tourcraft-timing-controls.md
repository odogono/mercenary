## Independent method and inputs

The ignored `campaign-timing-model.py` is a new tick/frame machine authored from the original reader facts. It reads the decoded campaign, outward-flight and shared-helper instructions independently extracted from pinned R6 RAM. Instruction boundaries, original PC/targets, opcode kinds, operands, availability bytes, entry offsets and return-stack contents come from that source. It does not use a retained list of expected request frames, branch outcomes or campaign completion times.

Each tick advances the unsigned wait, decrements the timer modulo 65,536 and advances the message busy/hold machine. Each atomic frame runs the recovered threshold/timed reader, pending boarding input, then the busy-gated interpreter and queue pickup in their recovered order. The model preserves availability-before-busy checks, repeatability, overwriteable queued IDs, direct bank-offset timers, nesting on the existing stack, flag changes, six-byte cash replacement and the signed encounter-request word. One decoded instruction executes per eligible invocation. The known frame-139 reentry repeats the reader/interpreter at the same tick; it is generated by the recovered flight phase rather than copied from the trace.

Boarding uses the naturally reached service precondition already proved by R6, with the actual nonzero cash-longword guard. It attempts nonqueueable pilot `$29` separately from playback start. The original launch path replaces the admission top's interpreter path, clears playback frame to zero and runs two eleven-tick waits before the first interpreter-bearing flight top at frame 2. The model retains this startup, the zero-tick space reentry, and recorded Leave at 927. It does not independently reimplement the craft arithmetic or encounter trajectories.

Starting VM state comes from the captured first top, with availability, hold and live return-stack entries from its pinned source RAM. The near-start probes begin at game tick 18,716 with two ticks already spent against boundary 18,714: the first partial wait is nine, then every modeled interval is eleven. Mining probes resume the machine produced by completed election, including real flag 9 and consumed availability; their first compared top is frame 3,048/tick 32,442. All five first compared tops have an idle bar; the model asserts this precondition.

External controls come from the debugger plans. Physical-VBL inputs are mapped to the first subsequent captured top as **input-delivery coordinates**, not campaign output expectations. These are B at VBL 23,500 for the two flight probes; the mining seconds write at 38,010; B at 38,050 for active-mining boarding; and B at 41,800 for cash refusal after mining. Election-on-foot supplies 32,400 seconds on restore. The two flight probes supply 32,400 or 62,000 at the explicit frame-100 breakpoint. No later PC, flag, queue, timer, cash or request observation drives a modeled transition.

Campaign threshold predicates use those supplied seconds and the previously proved clock bounds: the ordinary election path reaches the first threshold and remains below the second during these retained prefixes; sequential mining and the 62,000-second stress remain above the second. The model therefore does not feed sampled craft-rate writers or observed threshold starts into its expectations. Exact accelerated seconds/fraction arithmetic remains the separate strict-flight clock proof. Physical wall time and arbitrary clock-rate configurations are outside this comparison.

The interpreter's continuing-message field `$7230` is distinct from the message-source pointer at `$7232`. All used packed requests fit one row and these scenarios contain no continuing-message input; source and final checkpoint `$7230` are asserted zero. Message-counter seeding uses `$7286`. The native cash-refusal ID path is reduced to its previously recovered 251-tick busy lifetime; its raster/message pointer and sound phases are not newly modeled here.

All 4,908 tops and 4,905 interpreter entries match PC, flags, wait counter, timer bits, timed target, queued ID, cash and busy state, together with frame/tick phase; the second flag longword remains zero and is asserted throughout. Every one of the 3,124 executed opcode tuples, 139 request tuples and 170 mutating-opcode tuples matches PC/order/frame/tick. Every one of 3,143 ordinary launcher attempts matches script/frame/tick. The three sequential controls additionally trap direct timed dispatch: all four dispatch offsets/frame/ticks agree. The two queued-flight logs lack that dedicated trap; their calculated dispatches match the surrounding reader PC/target states and subsequent opcode sequence, rather than being represented as directly trapped calls.

The model asserts comparisons and fails on disagreement; printed summaries alone are not validation. All five final RAM checkpoints also agree in these VM fields, relevant availability bytes and the return-stack pointer/every live stack entry. The partial final waits are explicitly advanced to each checkpoint's stop tick (9/10/2/1/1 ticks respectively); no final field is used as a model update. `$56D8` is the low word of the current long pointer at `$56D6`, so original word writes preserve the existing stack cursor rather than resetting its base.

Consequential independently calculated timings agree with the original:

- On-foot election arms its first timer at frame 1,856/tick 19,330, dispatches at 1,993/20,837, arms its second at 2,090/21,904 and dispatches at 2,159/22,663. The encounter-request opcode executes at 2,228/23,422.
- Sequential mining arms 750 ticks at frame 3,175/tick 33,839, dispatches at 3,244/34,598, and writes cash during 3,280/34,994. Cash is first observed zero at the next top, 3,281/35,005. Boarding during mining changes these flight frame numbers to 123, 192, 228 and 229 respectively while retaining the same ticks.
- Queued election during flight begins in the threshold reader at 745/26,953, before the pilot's pending return. Its stale queue is picked up/refused at 813/27,701. Its first continuation begins at 938/29,076; the second timer is armed at 1,035/30,143 and remains pending when this retained prefix ends.
- In the 62,000-second stress, mining replaces the queued ID at 748/26,986, starts at 813/27,701, overwrites the election timer at 939/29,087, and dispatches its own continuation at 1,008/29,846. Cash writes at 1,044/30,242. This is a synthetic precedence stress, not a claim that the normal 28,800-game-second threshold separation naturally fits within the accepted flight.

These are action times. A following top that first observes the changed value is labeled separately. The native cash refusal and already-admitted zero-cash flight use the original guards; the model's matching behavior does not introduce an ejection or a forced pilot message.

## Boundary coverage and remaining limits

The five finite comparisons exercise consumed availability, nonqueueable pilot refusal, repeated threshold queue writes, queue replacement, unavailable queued pickup, a threshold launch between closing flag-clear and return, signed direct-target expiry, timer overwrite, cash replacement and boarding/cash-refusal interleavings. They cover all retained prefix events, including idle self-jumps; they do not assert that the partially retained election-flight prefix completes its pending final timer within the log.

The separate [original-engine boundary controls](mercenary-iii-tourcraft-boundary-controls.md) now confirm scheduler states absent from these normal scripts, including a due continuation with a busy bar but no active script, description-continuation priority and timer sign-window wrap. The [campaign handoff](mercenary-iii-tourcraft-campaign.md#scheduler-overwriteable-queue-and-signed-timer) proves why 32,768-tick active deferral is not a normal default-script outcome. Boundary controls remain synthetic tests of recovered semantics. Browser Save/Session Resume futures, R7 promotion/presentation and application regression are downstream requirements; RAM/model agreement does not certify them.
