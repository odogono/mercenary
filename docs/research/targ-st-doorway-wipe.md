# Escape from Targ ST: the Doorway wipe

Investigated 2026-11-05 for [#976](https://github.com/odogono/mercenary/issues/976), from the Atari ST release only (the disk pinned by [`research/targ-st/config/baseline.json`](../../research/targ-st/config/baseline.json)). The C64 build was not consulted. This is presentation evidence for the Faithful view, not Game Semantics.

Follow-up the same day: real Doorway crossings underground confirm the trigger chain, the direction rule and input handling. See [Natural crossings](#natural-crossings).

**The wipe is a palette raster split, not a fill.** The 3D view is drawn with palette entries 8–11. A Timer B interrupt, armed by the VBL handler, switches entries 8–11 at a chosen screen row. During a Doorway crossing the main loop stops inside its frame flip and moves that row 4 lines per VBL, from row 132 up to row 4 or from row 2 down to row 130. One side of the row shows a palette whose four entries all hold a Room's fill colour, so no lines show there. The other side shows a full palette, so lines show.

## Reproduction

```sh
cd research/targ-st
python3 -m targlab door-facts                 # byte-check every address below in E1
python3 -m targlab disasm 41AEE:41C64 41C64:41E16 48B10:48B72 432CA:4338E 42F76:42FAC 45040:4508A
python3 -m targlab confirm-door               # Hatari, about 4 minutes in real time
python3 -m targlab confirm-door-crossing      # ten real crossings in parallel, about 3 minutes
python3 -m targlab confirm-door-sound         # YM writes across a real up and down crossing (#978)
PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m unittest discover -s tests
```

`disasm` is the new standard-library 68000 disassembler, `targlab/m68k.py`. It reads `E1`, which loads at `$40000`; `--ram` reads a RAM dump instead. Every code address below lies in `E1`. Its bytes match the running program in the #743 in-play capture, except the saved vector at `$432F4`. `confirm-door` writes its RAM, screenshots, debugger scripts and `report.json` to `restricted/targ-st-door-976/` in the main checkout. It runs the #743 machine settings, except that fast-forward is off and `--frameskips 0` is set: with frames skipped, the screenshots showed stale splits. The installed Homebrew Hatari is 2.6.1 but a different build from the pinned hash. The probe checks the version and records the build's SHA-256 (`47de9d76…2331`).

`$44B94` runs when a Doorway's destination is Room 0. `$44B5A` then leaves the Room (`$623FC` ← 9), sets the surface palette and a fixed position (`x` = `$867C0`, `y` = `$40F480`, `z` = `$87000`), saves `$62448`, flips through `$48B72` (past the dispatch, so with no wipe) and restores the flag. The next flip, in the surface loop at `$48AB6`, runs the wipe on the first surface frame. The `exit` crossing confirmed this order: `$45078` (`$7F`), `$44B5A`, `$44B94`, `$44B9E`, then `$41AEE` five VBLs after the trigger, against two in a Room. Room `$AB` is the only Room in the table with such a Doorway. The player arrived about 64,865 high at location 08-08. Whether a normal game can reach it was not checked.

A Room whose Doorway list ends in `$80` rather than `$00` is dark. On entry `$44D6A..$44D92` sets palette entries 8, 10 and 11 to `$000` (entry 9 is already `$000` in Rooms), so the whole view is black. That happens unless bit 7 of `$631C4` is set, or the Room is the one in `$63144`. In the run `$631C4` was `$08` and `$63144` was `$4A`; what either one stands for was not recovered. The table has 20 dark Rooms. The `dark` crossing from Room 5 to Room `$49` ran `$44D80` on both entries. Its upward wipe ran in full (54 VBLs of freeze), but black on black, so it cannot be seen.

## Dynamic confirmation

`confirm-door` boots to VBL 6000, on foot and outdoors as in #743, where `$6244C` is already 0. It breaks at `$48B10`, writes `$62448` and a new palette (`$777, $357, $077, $000` into `$62438`), and continues. The outdoor sky uses entry 9 and the ground entry 10, so the split is visible across the whole view. The probe exercised the native code paths with controlled state. The [natural crossings](#natural-crossings) below cross real Doorways.

| Probe | Flag  | `Vsync` returns in the loop | VBLs (flip → first step → last step → main loop back) | Split row in screenshots                                                |
| ----- | ----- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| up    | `$7F` | 33                          | 6000 → 6002 → 6034 → 6055                             | new palette from row 104, 72, 40, 8 at samples d0 = 100, 68, 36, 4      |
| down  | `$FF` | 33                          | 6001 → 6027 → 6059 → 6060                             | flat new fill above row 30, 62, 94, 126 at samples d0 = 34, 66, 98, 130 |

A screenshot taken at the `Vsync` return shows the frame just completed, which used the previous value: up d0 + 4, down d0 − 4. Every sample places the split at exactly `$624E4`, at screenshot resolution (one ST row is two pixels; view row 0 is pixel row 58 with `--crop`). In the up probe the region above the split is uniformly old entry 10 (`#008800`), with sky and lines hidden. In the down probe it is uniformly new entry 10 (`#00EEEE`). The main-loop breakpoint `$48ABA` was armed at the poke and did not fire until the wipe and its hold had finished.

## Natural crossings

`confirm-door-crossing` boots ten cases in parallel to VBL 6000 and sends the player down a lift by jumping to `$47F92` with `d0` = a Room number. This skips only the pad and key test at `$47F30`. The native descent enters the Room through `$48140` and `$44B5A`. After 200 main-loop passes the probe sets `x`, `z` and the heading near a Doorway and holds the stick byte `$623B4`: bit 0 walks forward, bit 1 backward, bits 2 and 3 turn. It writes the byte the IKBD joystick vector would; nothing else is poked. The game then walks into the wall, clamps, matches the Doorway, changes Room and wipes. RAM, screenshots, debugger scripts, logs and `report.json` are in `restricted/targ-st-door-976/crossing/` in the main checkout.

**Position words.** In a Room, `x` (`$623A8`) runs west to east and `z` (`$623B0`) north to south. One grid cell is `$100`. The Room spans `0..width × $100` and `0..depth × $100` (record bytes 0 and 2; Room 1 is 16 × 16). The clamp holds the player between `$20` and the far wall minus `$20`. The heading `$6234C` is a 10-bit angle, `$100` per quarter turn. `$0000` walks toward −z (compass 0), `$0100` toward −x (compass 270), `$0200` toward +z (180) and `$0300` toward +x (90). Measured: 20 main-loop passes from (`$800`, `$800`) moved about `$D0` along that axis.

**Doorway records.** Each record is 4 bytes: flag, destination Room, then two cell bytes. Flag bit 7 picks an x-wall Doorway (west or east wall); clear means a z-wall Doorway (north or south wall). Bits 4–6 pick the Doorway you arrive at in the destination Room. Bit 3 marks a lock, and bits 0–3 then index the key table `$631BC`. An x-wall Doorway matches when `(x + $80) >> 8` is the wall (0 or the width), `z >> 8` is the cell, and `z`'s low byte is `$40..$BF`. A z-wall Doorway matches the other way round, with `x`'s low byte `$28..$A7`. The heading must also be within 45° of the wall's normal, facing either way. On arrival the player stands at the destination Doorway's cell, mid-cell along its wall, with the heading unchanged. `door.doorways` and `door.doorway_at` port the walk; the tests replay every case below.

The wall coordinate is below `$80` only at a north or west wall, and heading bit 1 is set only when the player faces north or west. The XOR is therefore 0 when the player faces the Doorway's wall and 1 when they face away. The `mid.png` screenshots show the sweeps described above on real Room geometry. Forward, the new Room's fill and lines rise from the bottom under the old fill, which is flat. Backward, the new fill falls from the top over the old frame, whose lines stay visible below the edge. The freeze is 54 VBLs from `$41AEE` to its end, and 58 for `$41C64`, as in the controlled probe.

**Keys during the freeze.** In the `keys-up` and `keys-down` cases the probe watches for a marker file written at `$41AEE` or `$41C64`. It then sends 4 or 6 'A' keypresses (scancode `$1E`) through Hatari's command FIFO, 0.05–0.47 s into the wipe. No key was read during the wipe. At its end (`$41C5A`, `$41E0C`) the EmuTOS keyboard iorec at `$30D4` held all 4 or 6, at buffer `$2F54`. After the wipe the main loop read every one at `$45344`, one per pass, every 2 VBLs (`keys-up`: VBLs 6724, 6726, 6728, 6730). Keys typed during a wipe are delayed, not lost. The buffer holds 64 entries, so a 1 s freeze cannot overflow it at typing speed. A held stick was not exercised through real IKBD packets, because Hatari's FIFO sends keys, not stick events.

## The door sound

Added 2026-11-05 for [#978](https://github.com/odogono/mercenary/issues/978), from the ST release only.

**Nothing starts a separate sound routine.** Each wipe writes the YM2149 itself, inline, at three points: before its first `Vsync`, between its two waits, and just before it returns. Every write is `move.b #r,$624EC` (the select shadow the VBL restores at `$43384` before `rte`), `move.b #r,$8800.w`, then the value to `$8802.w`. `door-facts` pins all six blocks (`upSoundOn`, `upSoundStep`, `upSoundOff`, `downSoundOn`, `downSoundStep`, `downSoundOff`), and `door.door_sound()` decodes them.

| Phase | Up `$41AEE`                                                                     | Down `$41C64`                                                    |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| On    | `$41AEE`: R4/R5 = `$890` (C), R2/R3 = `$980` (B), R10 = 12, R9 = 12, R7 = `$F8` | `$41C64`: the same periods, R9 = 12, R10 = 0 then 12, R7 = `$F8` |
| Held  | 33 Vsyncs (the sweep, rows 132 → 4)                                             | 25 Vsyncs (the hold)                                             |
| Step  | `$41BC2`: B = `$780`, C = `$680`                                                | `$41D0E`: C = `$780`, B = `$680`                                 |
| Held  | 21 Vsyncs (the hold)                                                            | 33 Vsyncs (the sweep, rows 2 → 130)                              |
| Off   | `$41C1C`: R9 = 0, R10 = 0, R7 ← `$624EA`                                        | `$41DCE`: R10 = 0, R9 = 0, R7 ← `$624EA`                         |

- **Two square-wave tones, no noise, no envelope.** Mixer `$F8` enables tones A, B and C and disables all noise. Channel A's volume stays 0: the VBL text-ticker path (`$43522`, `$4351A`) keeps writing R8 = 0. B and C sit at fixed volume 12 (bit 4 clear, so the envelope is unused; R11–R13 are not written). At the ST's 2 MHz PSG clock (f = 2,000,000 / (16 × period)) the chord is 51.4 Hz (`$980`) and 57.0 Hz (`$890`), then 65.1 Hz (`$780`) and 75.1 Hz (`$680`): a low two-step hum that rises once.
- **Up and down differ only in timing and channel order.** Up rises after 33 VBLs of 54; down after 25 of 58. Down swaps which channel takes `$780` and `$680`; with equal volumes that is the same sound. Down's extra `R10 = 0` before `R10 = 12` lasts one instruction.
- **The end restores the game's mixer.** R7 goes back to the shadow `$624EA` (`$CA` on both probed crossings), so any noise the game had on (engine or wind) returns; the wipe's `$F8` mutes it for the whole freeze.
- **Hatari confirms it.** `confirm-door-sound` reruns the `fz-max` and `bz-max` crossings with Hatari's `psg_write` trace on from the wipe's start to its end. Apart from the VBL's R8 = 0 (and one ticker write, R0 = `$40`, R8 = 12 then 0, on the up run), the only writes are the wipe's own, in the table's order and values. No VBL code writes R2–R5, R7, R9 or R10 during the freeze. Raw logs and `report.json` are in `restricted/targ-st-door-976/sound/`; `tests/test_door.py` holds the traced sequence and checks it against the decoded bytes.
- **Unconfirmed.** Frame counts in the trace come from `video_cyc` wrapping, not a VBL counter, and are approximate; the hold lengths come from the loop bytes and the 54/58 VBL freeze measured above. The probe logs writes, not audio; the sound was not recorded or listened to.

## Against the user's capture

The capture (4 fps, 60 Hz) shows dark red → cyan rising from the bottom, cyan → magenta falling from the top, and magenta → cyan rising again. That matches two wipe paths picked per crossing. The rising cyan region shows the new Room's lines, and the old colour above it is flat. The falling sweep keeps the old Room's vertical edge below the edge. A sweep spans two or three samples, as 33 VBLs at 60 Hz predicts. By the confirmed rule, the player walked forward into the cyan Room, backed into the magenta one, and walked forward again (or turned round first).

## Open questions

- **The lift was jumped, not walked.** Every case enters its first Room through the native descent, but by a jump to `$47F92` rather than standing on a lift pad and pressing the key (`$12`, 'E', `$453E4 → $47EC6`). Doorway crossings themselves are native.
- **A real stick.** Movement was driven by writing `$623B4`, the byte the joystick vector writes. A stick moved or released during the freeze through IKBD packets was not exercised.
- **Locked Doorways.** No case crossed a locked Doorway (record bit 3), so the `$4508A` message path and the `$631E4` override are static.
- **Dark-Room exceptions.** What `$631C4` bit 7 (a lamp?) and the Room number in `$63144` stand for was not recovered.
- **Room `$AB`'s exit.** Whether a normal game reaches it, and what the fixed position it sets is, were not checked.
