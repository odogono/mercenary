# Choose the Destination in an authored Departure

One app will eventually hold more than one game: _Escape from Targ_ today, _The Second City_ through the Content Overlay seam, Damocles after it inside the multi-title Independent Implementation (ADR 0021), and — as aspiration only — _The Dion Crisis_. Something has to ask "which game?", and today nothing does: boot goes straight into the Arrival, New Game replays it, and `createWalkabout` takes exactly one Targ-shaped bundle. This ADR settles the shape of that question before _The Second City_ builds its seam toward the wrong answer: the player chooses by choosing **where to fly**, in an authored, shell-owned **Departure** that precedes the unchanged Arrival.

The Departure opens in a deep-space star field carrying a flat carousel of **Destinations** — a Destination being one playable experience, named by its system, its world, and where the player will land. The carousel rotates left and right and snaps per entry; there is no nesting and no sub-menu. Committing an entry starts one continuous camera journey — interstellar travel, a flyby past the chosen system's bodies, then convergence on the planet-free opening state of the Arrival Field — that ends, without a cut, immediately before the Arrival's `titleCard` phase. The `M E R C E N A R Y` title card remains the Arrival's first beat, and the Arrival alone owns the later planet approach. The Arrival itself (ADR 0032) is otherwise untouched: its narration, its held session, its skip proof, its handover at the status report.

Naming the choice a Destination rather than a title is deliberate. The selectable things are mechanically heterogeneous — _The Second City_ is a Content Overlay on Targ, Damocles a sibling title with its own payload family — and ADR 0021 explicitly deferred whether that overlay story later flattens into "titles". The Destination keeps it deferred: to the player, `TARG` and `TARG — SECOND CITY` are sibling entries on one carousel, and how their content is composed underneath says nothing the carousel needs to know. When _The Second City_ ships, the two Targ cities appear as two islands during the Arrival's planet approach, but that is **approach fiction only**, dressing a choice already committed on the carousel: the game surface stays the Reference Build's wrapping 16×16 world (ADR 0034), with no coastline, no shared two-city geography, and no flying from one city to the other.

The Departure is Browser Shell presentation in exactly ADR 0032's mold. Nothing here is Session State, nothing enters a Save Snapshot, and a Gameplay Session begins only when a Destination is committed. It shows on first boot when there is no Session Resume and on every New Game behind the existing two-press confirm; a Session Resume bypasses it entirely. It plays under both Presentation Presets, on ADR 0032's own argument: no original release had it, so there is nothing recovered for Authentic to be faithful to, and presenting nothing would assert something false. The honesty lives in the rosters instead: **every celestial body a flyby shows is bound to an authority** — Targ's flyby to the Targ Orrery's sun and two moons, Damocles's to the Gamma System roster decoded as Working-Authority Evidence (`docs/research/damocles-gamma-system.md`) — and invented bodies are prohibited. The Arrival Narration's own "DESTINATION-GAMMA FIVE" is the fictional stitch between the two.

The carousel offers **only what exists**. Initial Release shows a one-entry carousel — the starship dressing around New Game — and each shipped release adds an entry; a locked or teased entry is a promise with a date on it, and the second island simply _appearing_ on approach is a better announcement than any padlock. _The Dion Crisis_ stays non-binding aspiration with no selector treatment: it has no research standing in this repository, and it shares Damocles's Gamma System, which is precisely why this design must not hard-code "one system = one Destination".

Skipping preserves ADR 0032's provable invariant by composition: the carousel is initially snapped to the last played Destination — Targ, original city, on first boot — and the skip key **commits the currently snapped entry** and fast-forwards the journey and the Arrival to the join. Skip is "commit current and fast-forward", never "escape to some default", so a first-time player still reaches the status report with zero decisions, and skipping and watching still reach the identical Save Snapshot.

Saves gain a Destination. A Save and the Session Resume name the Destination they belong to, and a Save restores only into a session of the same Destination. The Session Resume stays a **single** record — one current game at a time — so committing a different Destination is New Game, behind the same confirm it already has. _(Amended by ADR 0041: the Session Resume is one record per Title, not one for the app.)_ Nothing changes today: the Destination field lands in Save identity when the second Destination ships, not before.

This ADR is a north-star design, not a build. Under ADR 0021 nothing multi-title loads until after _The Second City_; the deliverables now are this decision, the non-loaded sketch at `docs/research/departure-destination-sketch.md`, and the glossary terms **Departure** and **Destination**. Its near-term payoff is constraint: _The Second City_ is surfaced as a second carousel entry with its own Save identity, not as a mode, a menu toggle, or a second app.

_(Amended 2026-09-08, issue #160: the **one-entry Departure ships early**, as Browser Shell presentation at the app's single address. It loads no second Title and changes no Save schema, so it stays inside ADR 0021; what it proves early are the three hard parts — the seamless join into the unchanged Arrival, Departure-first boot with a Session Resume bypassing it, and the skipped-equals-watched Save Snapshot by composition. Three details settled at build time differ from the paragraphs above and from the sketch. **Skip exists only after commit:** the carousel offers a Commit alone, taken by Enter, Space or its button; a Skip control in page chrome appears for the journey and fast-forwards journey and Arrival together to the status report, then vanishes at the join. Space is inert during the journey, so the one key keeps one meaning per beat — commit from deep space, hurry Benson once the Arrival has begun. "Skip commits the snapped entry" is therefore no longer the mechanism; the invariant it served survives unchanged, since committing the only entry is not a decision and skipped and watched still reach the identical Save Snapshot. **The Departure is silent**, as the prototype was. **Reduced motion** is honoured automatically from `prefers-reduced-motion`, with no Settings entry. Path routing, the last-played Destination and the Destination field on Saves remain deferred as written.)_

## Considered Options

- A free-flight starship between systems, with a flight model and travel time.
- A session-owned playable prologue, with deep space in Saves and Resume.
- Nested selection: snap to a system, then choose a city inside it.
- An interactively steered descent, where drifting toward an island picks the city.
- Visible-but-locked carousel entries for unshipped games.
- A shared two-city world: both cities on one surface, islands as real geography.
- Gating the Departure behind Enhanced presentation.
- A flat, shell-owned, preset-blind carousel of existing Destinations, committed before an authored journey into the unchanged Arrival.

The last option is taken. Free flight drags a Craft Parameter Block and a Mover into shell chrome, which are Gameplay Session concepts. A playable prologue invents a meta-session none of the originals had and breaks Save identity for the sake of resuming a menu. Nesting buys nothing at three entries. A steered descent destroys the skip-equals-watch proof and lands ambiguously between cities. Locked entries date the app. A shared two-city world is a new game rather than a Faithful Remake — it breaks ADR 0034's surface-geometry rule and the Content Overlay model both. And Enhanced-gating fails because the carousel is functional: it is _how you choose_, so Authentic players must see it too.

## Consequences

- ADR 0021's sequencing is untouched: nothing multi-title loads until after _The Second City_, and this ADR ships as design artifacts only.
- The Departure delivers the exact planet-free opening state of the Arrival Field and hands over before `titleCard`; the title card and planet approach remain the Arrival's.
- **Departure** and **Destination** enter the glossary; the deep-space chooser is never called a title screen, main menu, or launcher.
- Save identity gains a Destination field when the second Destination ships — no Save schema change now, and the Session Resume stays one record.
- The Second City's player-facing surface is decided: a sibling carousel entry and a second island on approach, whatever the overlay mechanics beneath.
- Flyby rosters are evidence-bound (Targ Orrery; decoded Gamma System), so the Departure adds no invented astronomy to defend later.
- _The Dion Crisis_ remains non-binding; nothing here reserves it a system, an entry, or a seam.
