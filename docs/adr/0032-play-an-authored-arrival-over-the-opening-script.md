# Play an authored Arrival over the opening script

The game opens in space. A star field, the panel live below it with its readouts still rows of asterisks, Benson counting a Novadrive down and then reporting the failure that follows; Targ appearing as a dot in the middle of the field and growing until it is the whole screen; a fall onto the airstrip at 8:8 with the altimeter running down from eighty-two thousand; a blackout at impact; and then the crash site, the status report, and a Dart for sale. This is the **Arrival**, and it is an authored addition rather than a recovered one.

It has to be authored, because the Reference Build does not have it. The configured build is the ABC/1001 crack, and the intro proper was stripped from it: `notes/decode/benson-scripts.md` records that the opening script is started at launch because nothing in the disassembly says how the engine reached it, and the first thing the laboratory has ever observed is already the in-world wireframe view. The original releases did have it — the 8-bit original boots straight into the star field, and the 16-bit releases add a title screen in front of the same sequence — so what is authored here is a recreation by eye and by ear of something the game had, not an invention of something it did not. That is the same standing as ADR 0027's instrument panels, which are recreated rather than extracted because the original artwork is Restricted Material, and the same honesty ADR 0025 asks of the Enhanced sky. `docs/fidelity-contract.md` already anticipates this shape: an intro absent from the configured artifact is an `excluded-crack-artifact`, retained with its provenance outside conformance rather than claimed as fidelity.

**The commentary is the opening Benson Script's own, and nothing new is written for it.** Script 00 already plays "CRASH IMMINENT," then "RETURNING TO - - -" and "MANUAL CONTROL", picks one of four landing verdicts at random, and delivers the status report from "STATUS REPORT" and "CRASH LANDED ON TARG" onward. Those are the last ten seconds of the original's minute, and they are decoded behaviour. So the Arrival presents *over* them rather than replacing them: the descent's altitudes are anchored to those lines, the verdict is the impact — which is what the original's stills show, an "OUCH!" at zero altitude — and the status report is the join, where the Arrival ends and the game becomes the player's. Game Semantics are untouched, and the state the Arrival hands over is the state the game has always started in.

The seventeen lines *before* "CRASH IMMINENT," are not in the Data Payload and cannot be, because they were in the stripped part. They are **Arrival Narration**: authored shell-side text, quoted verbatim from the original releases, played through the same message line by a shell-owned Benson Display. That display is independent of the Gameplay Session but uses the same persistent 23 cells, 25-character bursts and 50 Hz scanout as the in-game one; the Arrival's pacing is expressed through its silent interval and reading hold rather than through a second progressive ticker. This is a step beyond ADR 0027 and is named as such — a panel recreated by eye reproduces no bytes, whereas these are the original's own words. They are quoted because paraphrase would be a worse recreation, they are seventeen short strings rather than artwork or code, and they are marked in the source as authored so nothing downstream can mistake them for decoded text. Deliberately they are not Benson Messages: nothing here reaches the interpreter, the Host Callout or the message transcript, and the glossary's exclusion of shell text from "Benson Message" is honoured rather than stretched.

**The Arrival plays under both Presentation Presets**, which inverts ADR 0025's rule and is the one place this decision needs arguing. Authentic presentation retains the accepted Reference Build appearance — but the Reference Build's *absence* of an opening is an artifact of the medium it was cracked from, not an appearance anyone accepted, and there is no recovered opening for Authentic to be faithful to. Presenting nothing under Authentic would make the preset assert something about the original that is false. The Arrival Field is therefore identical under both presets, as ADR 0031's Interstellar Ship Field is, though for the opposite reason: that field is preset-blind because it is recovered, and this one because there is nothing recovered to differ from. The descent segment is the ordinary world renderer, so it picks up the Enhanced sky exactly as play does.

**Nothing here is Session State.** The Arrival is Browser Shell presentation over an unmodified Gameplay Session, and the Save schema is untouched at 8. This is the deliberate opposite of ADR 0031, whose reasoning does not transfer: the Interstellar Ship Field had to be Session State because the Ending Phase's advance is written by the field's own colour sequence and because a Save taken mid-presentation must continue it exactly. An authored opening writes nothing and decides nothing; making it Session State would bump the schema to 9 and, since the codec has never had a migration, refuse every existing Save for the sake of resuming a cinematic. A reload during the Arrival therefore begins it again rather than resuming it halfway. Two consequences follow and are owned rather than hidden: nothing is checkpointed while the field is up, because the session is not being advanced then, so a reload before the handover is indistinguishable from a first boot; and a reload after it resumes the opening script where the Session Resume left it, re-entering the Arrival at the descent milestone that script has reached, which replays the fall but not the narration.

**The opening script is held rather than deferred.** The walkabout starts script 00 exactly as it always did, and the Arrival withholds *time* instead of withholding the start: the session receives no steps at all until the handover. Starting the script late was tried first and is wrong, because a Save taken while the star field is up would then restore a game whose opening script had never run — no report, no argument, no Dart to buy. Holding a started script has the same visible behaviour, since the script cannot speak without being stepped, and it means every Save taken during the Arrival restores a game that still has its opening to play.

**Skipping is the same game as watching, and that is provable rather than asserted.** One key — the one that already hurries Benson along — finishes the narration at once and then runs the opening script to the join in the same fixed steps the frame loop would have given it. Because the session is idle until the handover, both paths hand the same waiting script the identical run of 1/60-second steps, so the state at the join is the same state, random landing verdict included. A unit test compares the two Save Snapshots rather than trusting the argument.

The Arrival holds the player's keys while it presents, including the few seconds of ground view the landing verdict is read over: the game becomes the player's at the status report, not at the impact. The System Menu and the Shell Console stay reachable throughout, because an opening a player cannot get out of would be worse than one they can skip. While the Arrival owns the message line it shows its own text ahead of a Session Resume notice — a standing refusal is still repeated when the menu is opened, so nothing is lost, only deferred.

Two smaller departures from the original are recorded here because they are visible. The dive is about thirty-five degrees rather than the original's near-vertical plunge: the decoded projection hangs its ground fill off the horizon, and past roughly sixty degrees the horizon is far enough above the frame that the fill no longer reaches it, so a steeper fall would be a plunge through an empty sky. And the Arrival runs on its own pacing rather than the game's — a settings block of its own, defaulting to about sixty-five seconds from title card to touchdown, which is the original's measured length. That block is deliberately outside the settings that identify a Save, so retuning the opening cannot make an existing Save unloadable. Its one awkwardness is that the shell swaps the session's script pacing at the handover and swaps it back at the verdict, which is presentation reaching for a knob that belongs to the session; a `retimeScripts` action on the Gameplay Session would be the tidier seam and is deferred rather than pretended away.

Judged, as all visual fidelity here is, by the maintainer playing it beside the original recording (ADR 0011). There is no Trace Oracle for an opening the frozen Swift build never had, and no Golden Trace to compare against.
